They packed half a ton of dynamite under a dead whale, and the engineer pushed the button. You see, in 1970, a whale washed up on a beach in Oregon. Forty five feet long, eight tons, and it stank for miles. Nobody knew whose job that was, so it went to the highway department, because out there, the beach counts as a road. Their plan was simple. Blow it into tiny pieces, and let the seagulls eat the rest. One guy in the crowd actually knew explosives. He told them twenty sticks would do it. They used twenty cases. So everyone backed up a quarter mile, a news crew started filming, and boom. For one second, it was beautiful. Then the sky started falling. Chunks of whale three feet wide came down on the crowd, and one of them crushed a brand new car. Guess whose car it was. The guy who said twenty sticks. And the seagulls? The blast scared every single one away. Most of the whale was still lying there, so they buried it anyway. Today, Oregon has one rule for dead whales. Bury them where they land. And the engineer? He said it went exactly right.
