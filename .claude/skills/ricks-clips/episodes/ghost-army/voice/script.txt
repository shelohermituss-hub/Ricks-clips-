Four soldiers pick up a thirty-ton tank... and set it down in a field like a suitcase! Two Frenchmen on bicycles stop dead. A soldier winks: "Americans are very strong." You see, that tank is made of rubber. And these soldiers are painters, set designers and art students! Their job: make the enemy believe a whole army is here... when there's nobody. One rule: nobody gets closer than a quarter mile. Up close, it's all fake. From far away, it's an army! By day they blow up tanks, trucks and cannons, and a bulldozer even draws the track marks! At night, one truck rolls by... and you hear a hundred tanks! Engines, tracks, shouted orders, all from a speaker you can hear fifteen miles away! On the radio, they even copy the way real operators tap their Morse code, like forging a signature. And in the village café, they sew on fake patches and let slip fake secrets... for the spies. A real sergeant laughs: "So now we fight wars with carnival balloons?" Then comes the big river. In six days, the real army has to cross it. So the ghost army blows up six hundred tanks, ten miles to the south. The enemy photographs them, listens to them, spies on them... and sends every gun at the balloons! Meanwhile, up north, the real army crosses the river with barely a shot fired. You see, it's the pillow-under-the-blanket trick: while everyone watches the bed, the kid is already outside. And the last people they fooled? Their own families. The secret lasted more than fifty years.
